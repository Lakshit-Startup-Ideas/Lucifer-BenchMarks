# Literature review (14 Nov 2026): what was checked, and what was not

## Status: partial, abstract level
- Every source below was **confirmed to exist** by a web search on 14 Nov 2026 (title, authors, venue and year as returned by the search). **None was read in full.** The one-line summaries come from abstracts and search snippets. Where my use of an idea differs from the original, the difference is stated.
- No claim of V1.1 depends on a source being read. Nothing here is a validation of a V1.1 result.
- The brief names a book as a foundation (Gupta, Jin, Homma). **No chapter of it was read.** V1.1 does not follow it; any statement that it does would be false.
- Topics the brief asks about that are **not reviewed**: plasticity rules (Hebbian, STDP), complementary learning systems, neural development and evolution, spiking and continuous-time models beyond what is noted, event-sourcing literature, preference learning and reward modelling. See issue "Read the cited sources in full and fill the literature gaps".

## Biological analogies used (the brief's five-part form)
| Mechanism | Biological explanation | Mathematical abstraction in V1.1 | Implementation | Evidence for the abstraction | Limits of the analogy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Goal maintenance in prefrontal cortex (source 8) | Patterns of activity representing goals are actively maintained and bias other structures | A goal stored outside a limited working memory; evicting distractors first; rebuilding a lost subgoal from the goal | `m3.py` (`WorkingMemory`, goal slot) | **Only** that the mechanism behaves as specified in a designed world (M3); no biological evidence | Operational definition. The brain is not one buffer, and nothing here tests prefrontal function |
| Synaptic consolidation (source 3) | Important synapses are stabilised against later change | A pull of the shared weights toward their previous values | `learner.py` (`snapshot_anchor`) | Reduced forgetting in a small simulation (M4) | Uniform, not importance-weighted; one strength; no claim about real synapses |
